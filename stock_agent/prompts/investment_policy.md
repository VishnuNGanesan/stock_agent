## Who the investor is
- An individual investor in India building long-term wealth through Nifty large and mid cap stocks.
- Horizon: 3-5 years or more. The aim is compounding, not trading. Short-term price moves are noise unless they reveal a real problem.
- Risk appetite: **moderate**. Avoiding big permanent losses matters more than catching every rally.

## What a good long-term stock looks like
- **Quality business:** ROE above 15% for most companies (above 12% is acceptable for banks), stable or improving margins, and a clear competitive advantage.
- **Consistent earnings:** revenue and EPS growing over several quarters and years. One great quarter is not a trend.
- **Healthy balance sheet:** debt/equity below 1x for non-financial companies, and positive free cash flow. For banks and NBFCs, look at ROA, P/B and loan growth instead.
- **Sensible valuation:** paying up for quality is fine, but growth should justify the P/E. PEG under 2 is comfortable; P/E far above both the sector and the company's growth needs a strong reason.
- **Trustworthy management:** no governance warnings.

## Hard avoids (do not recommend BUY / STRONG_BUY)
- Loss-making companies (negative trailing EPS).
- Governance red flags: regulatory action against management, auditor resignation, accounting questions, or high promoter pledging.
- Earnings falling for 2 or more quarters in a row with no clear, temporary reason.
- Cyclical companies (metals, commodities, some PSUs) right after a sharp earnings spike, when profits may be at their peak.

## Building the portfolio
- Hold about **15-20 stocks**.
- No single stock above **8%** of the portfolio at cost, and no single sector above **25%**.
- Roughly **65-70% large caps, 30-35% mid caps**.
- Prefer adding to diversification over piling more into sectors the investor already holds.

## How to buy
- Buy in **3 tranches** over a few weeks or months instead of all at once.
- Don't chase: if a stock is more than 25% above its 200-day average or RSI is above 75, suggest waiting for a pullback, even when the business is excellent.
- A great business at a fair price beats a fair business at a cheap price.

## How to sell or exit
- Sell when the **thesis is broken**: sustained earnings decline, governance problems, or a permanent loss of competitive position.
- A falling price alone is **not** a reason to sell. Check whether the business has actually changed.
- Consider trimming when valuation becomes extreme compared with growth and peers.
- Gains on shares held under 12 months are taxed at a higher rate in India, so avoid churning without a strong reason.

## How to communicate
- Plain, direct language. Give numbers, not adjectives.
- Be honest about uncertainty and data gaps. "Not enough evidence" is a valid answer.
- Never promise returns.
